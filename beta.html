What to Expect

  • This is a solar clock, and measures the time of day relative to the sun at your location.
  • You will notice that the dial is divided into sixteen segments. Twelve equal-length segments, the solar hours, denote the time from sunrise and sunset; while four equal-length segments, the watches, denote the time from sunset to sunrise.
  • The exact size of the hour and watch segments depends both on your location and your time of year. Hours will appear longer in the summer and shorter in the winter, while watches appear shorter in the summer and longer in the winter.
  • The top of the dial is fixed to solar midday (when the sun is at it's zenith) at your location.
  • The solar hour hand (the golden hand with three bars) will transcribe the full rotation of the dial smoothly from one midday to the next, while the segments it crosses will adjust to reflect the time of day.

What to Test

  • Is the solar hour hand accurately tracking the sun's movement at your location?
  • Does the length of the day hours and night watches accurately reflect the length of day and night where you are?
  • Does the splash screen behave as expected?
  • Is the interface clear and consistent across your device?
  • Does the solar dial continue to display correctly several hours after passing midnight or midday if the app is left running on the background?
